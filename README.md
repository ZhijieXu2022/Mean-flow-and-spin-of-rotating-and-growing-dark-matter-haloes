# The mean flow, velocity dispersion, energy transfer and evolution of rotating and growing dark matter haloes

Xu Z., 2026, *The mean flow, velocity dispersion, energy transfer and evolution of rotating and growing dark matter haloes*, Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.24095667](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24095667). The preprint is hosted on arXiv as [arXiv:2201.12665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12665). This record is a new version of the earlier record [zenodo.org/records/6640380](https://zenodo.org/records/6640380).

## Major findings

- The angular momentum of a halo changes only through the flux of matter across its boundary; internal radial flows move angular momentum between shells but cannot create it. The rotational kinetic energy changes by the same boundary flux plus a production term, 2ρu<sub>φ</sub><sup>2</sup>u<sub>r</sub>/r, the analogue of Reynolds-stress production in turbulence.
- For a halo whose mass and radius grow in proportion to time, the azimuthal equation has an exact separable solution, u<sub>φ</sub> ∝ sin<sup>α<sub>θ</sub></sup>θ M(&lt;r)/r. It predicts a specific angular momentum of spherical shells proportional to the enclosed mass (j ∝ M), a solid-body core, and a halo angular momentum H<sub>h</sub> = γ<sub>H</sub> H r<sub>h</sub><sup>2</sup> with γ<sub>H</sub> = 0.43–0.51, i.e. a spin parameter λ′ = 0.033–0.038 that does not depend on mass or time.
- On 2.0 × 10<sup>6</sup> haloes of an N-body simulation at z = 0–1, the sampling noise λ<sub>noise</sub> ≈ 0.31 n<sub>p</sub><sup>−1/2</sup> is more than half of the measured spin for haloes with fewer than about 150 particles. Haloes resolved with more particles give s = 0.92–1.11, γ<sub>H</sub> = 0.49–0.66 and λ′ = 0.037–0.050, close to the solution.
- Rotation carries only 0.9–1.5 per cent of the kinetic energy and is concentrated to the equatorial plane (α<sub>θ</sub> ≈ 1.1–1.5). The radial velocity anisotropy is the same in the non-rotating counterparts of the haloes, so it is not induced by spin.
- The mean radial flow of simulated haloes is inward at all resolved radii, so rotation is spun up throughout the halo by contraction.
- At late times, when the concentration grows at a fixed inner structure, the Peebles spin parameter increases while the Bullock spin parameter decreases.
